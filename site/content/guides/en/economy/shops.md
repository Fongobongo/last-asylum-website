## VIP Shop: Diamond Priorities {#vip-shop}

The VIP shop takes diamonds, and most of its stock is a bad trade. The verified buy list:

| Priority | Items |
|---|---|
| HIGH | UR Omnifragment, UR Antidote, Crow Essence, 8h Speedup |
| MEDIUM | 3h Speedup, SSR Omnifragment, 1h Speedup |
| LOW | SSR Antidotes, Direct Relocation |

And the full rule: **avoid everything else unless extreme necessity.** The shop is priced to tempt impatient players into converting diamonds into items the game gives away through events — hold the line.

### The 10,000-Diamond Monthly VIP — the Best Diamond Investment in the Game

The **Monthly VIP activation (10,000 diamonds)** is the single strongest diamond purchase available to any player, F2P included:

- It banks that diamond spend into VIP progress that unlocks permanent economy and stat perks (extra trade, research speed, commodity perks) that no shop item can match for the price.
- It stacks with the battle pass and daily packs — the 10K you spend here is saved, not spent.
- **Renew it every month.** Missing a month is the classic F2P diamond-mistake: you grind back to 10K slower next time because you lost a month of boosted income.

In the diamond-vs-item trade-off, Monthly VIP wins against every shop item; buy it first, then shop priorities above.

### Spending Diamonds to Push VIP Levels — Sometimes It's Correct

Each VIP level does more than look pretty: it unlocks new permanent account perks **and new rows in the VIP shop**. The "spend diamonds only on items" instinct is wrong here — from VIP 6 onward, every new tier adds a new shop slot (being able to buy 1h/3h/8h speedups in the VIP Shop alone pays the diamond cost back within two weeks), and the highest tiers stack research and training speed the ordinary shop doesn't carry.

Practical read: if pushing VIP 6→7 takes 5–8K paid diamonds, spending them on the push is strictly better than spending them on a banner cycle — the unlock lasts forever, the banner ends.

## Shop Restock Schedule {#restock-schedule}

All shops restock on Monday. Plan your week around it: buy out your priorities Monday when stocks are full, run your Alliance Duel and Expedition income mid-week, then spend the weekend deciding what Monday's refresh owes you. Players who shop on Sunday buy a picked-clean shelf.

## Epigraph Chests: The Exception Rule {#epigraph-chests}

Buy epigraph chests in every shop **except the Honor Shop**. Epigraphs feed the Raven system, and the Raven's UR epigraphs only buff one faction — in the mono-faction meta, that makes epigraph chests the single most consistent long-term purchase in the game.

Free-to-play epigraph income is real, if you show up for it: 30 UR shards per day from Alliance Duel, plus self-forging in the Epigraph Workshop, adds up to roughly 1.5 UR epigraphs per month. The full system — workshop levels, promotion order, and rarity rules — is covered in the [Raven guide](/raven/).

## Diamond Rules: Wheels, Banners, and Milestones {#diamond-rules}

1. **Forest for the trees: 10,000 diamonds → Monthly VIP.** Behind any wheel math, the best single diamond buy in the game is the 10K Monthly VIP — full stop.
2. **Save until day 8** for the Cynthia Wishing Wheel — 7 free daily spins + 10-spin milestone (10 fragments = full copy) mean the real cost is ~1,500 diamonds in top-up spins. Stop after the milestone.
3. **After day 15, save for Joker's wheel** — the UR PvP finisher event. The mid-game diamond reserve exists for this.
4. **Never dump diamonds into standard recruitment.** Wait for rate-up banners — same currency, better heroes.
5. **Do not chase cumulative diamond-spend milestones** like Deep Sea's Gift. Spending diamonds just to cross a reward threshold is buying the milestone, not the value.

## Smart Spending: Aim Every Purchase {#smart-spending}

![Planned spending strategy in Last Asylum](/img/dtf/economy/01.jpg)

Early on the game carries you by itself — tap, collect, unlock — but past the first stretch the same routine stops converting into power: PvP losses pile up, upgrades stretch for hours, sessions stall on empty energy. Trying to brute-force through it with more logins changes almost nothing. Spending starts helping only when it stops being random. The rule experienced players converge on is simple: **it is not how much you put in, but exactly where**. Every purchase should answer one question: *what am I strengthening right now?*

Around the mid-levels the pressure comes from all sides: there seem to be more resources, yet something specific is always missing — materials for a hero or time for the base — while rivals already look stronger. That is where the familiar urge to "buy just a little" leads to the classic mistakes: lucky-dip packs, collecting random heroes, flip-flopping between PvP and PvE. The result is an account that grows but is strong nowhere: no damage in PvP, no stability in PvE. The turning point is focus on a single goal — arena or content — so that every investment works toward it and the game starts "answering back" with steadier wins.

### The priority ladder

- **Starter and special packs first.** The fastest way to feel the difference: a strong hero plus base resources at once makes PvE stable immediately, with no repeated stalls on ordinary stages.
- **Energy second.** It looks minor at first, but energy decides how much you actually play — farming, missions, base growth. With enough of it, progress turns from accidental into regular.
- **Subscriptions and daily bonuses third.** This is about stability: a baseline of resources every login, easier upgrade planning, no dropping out of tempo.
- **Mid-game: rare heroes and gear-upgrade materials.** Exactly the things that move both PvE and PvP noticeably.
- **Late game: seasonal packs and speedups.** They keep you at the top where ordinary farming no longer pulls its weight.

![Steady, sensible spending that speeds up progress](/img/dtf/economy/02.jpg)

### What to skip

- **Cosmetics** — zero effect on combat.
- **Random chests** — occasional luck, mostly filler that creates an illusion of progress with no real gain.
- **Small short-lived boosts** — a real effect for one evening, nearly invisible over a month.
- **Duplicate heroes that feed nothing** — if they do not strengthen the roster, they are decoration.

Cutting these removes the scatter: spending gets even and controllable, and every investment is felt.

### Events are the growth point

Events hold the rewards that are hardest to farm by standard means — rare materials, useful items, sometimes strong heroes. The approach that pays off: prepare in advance instead of walking in cold, do not cap your own attempts (bring the tickets), finish quests fully rather than halfway, and claim all key rewards. Timed this way, spending squeezes the maximum out of a specific moment instead of merely "speeding something up".

![Timing spending with events for maximum rewards](/img/dtf/economy/03.jpg)

### Smart-spending checklist

1. Starter packs and energy first — a strong hero and a steady resource flow.
2. Mid-game, aim at rare heroes and gear materials.
3. Late game, seasonal packs and speedups for the top.
4. Save on trifles: cosmetics, random chests, micro-boosts.
5. Plan around events — the maximum return per investment lands there.
