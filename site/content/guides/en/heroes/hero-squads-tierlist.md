Stats are taken at full investment (level and star template, personal growth ratio, awakening and exclusive gear). Over a 30-second fight every skill casts on its cooldown, the first cast at its initial delay. Survivability = (HP + shields + healing) / (1 − damage reduction, capped at 75%). ## Tools

A squad in Last Asylum: Plague is made of five heroes, and how those five are matched matters more than the Might of any one of them. This page ranks all 169,911 possible five-hero squads by tier. They were judged not by total Might but by what they do in a fight: how much the squad withstands and how hard it hits. The calculation itself is explained on How squads are rated; this page keeps to the results and what they mean for a player. The best squad from the heroes actually owned is computed by the squad builder, and the arena team builder checks it against a specific opponent.

## How a squad works

The five heroes stand in two rows: the front row takes the hits, the back row deals damage from behind. A tank with nobody behind is therefore as useless as five shooters with no tank. Every hero leads soldiers, and how many is set by the hero's Leadership: the higher it is, the bigger the army and the longer the squad survives. Leadership grows with hero levels and is topped up by buildings.

Each hero belongs to a faction: warrior, warlock or ranger. Warrior beats warlock, warlock beats ranger, ranger beats warrior, and the difference is 20% damage either way, so a squad is also picked against the enemy it expects to meet. A squad built from one faction gets a shared bonus to HP, ATK and DEF: three heroes of one faction give +5%, three plus two give +10%, four of one faction +15%, all five of one faction +20%. Among the multipliers that depend purely on the line-up, this one is the biggest.

There are three roles, and the role sits right in the hero card as its own icon, so there is no need to guess from stats. Tanks grow into HP and hold the front line, DPS heroes grow into ATK and kill, and support sits between them: more HP than DPS heroes, almost as much ATK. Support is not a weak DPS hero but a separate role with an exclusive job: every skill that heals allies belongs to it, and no tank and no DPS hero in the game heals at all. Three of the five support heroes heal, the other two give shields and damage reduction. Support decides not how hard the squad hits but how many fights in a row it survives without going home.

Five tanks live long but kill nothing, five DPS heroes delete the first target if they live that long, and five support heroes do neither. The answer to that choice is computed below: the working shape rests on three tanks, and the last two places go either to two DPS heroes or to a DPS hero and a support. Spare heroes are never idle either: the support skill only works while the hero stands outside the main squad, so the bench buffs the team from where it sits.

The same table for players who do not buy heroes. Three cases are covered: no UR at all, reachable UR, and one UR of any kind. Total Might is lower, but the faction bonus and squad-wide skills work just the same.

Reachable UR here means Marlena and Arthur, and what makes them budget heroes differs. Marlena is the only UR who accumulates for free: development goals alone are guaranteed to pay 920 of her shards, about 940 guaranteed free in total. For comparison, Cynthia, the next one down, has 99 guaranteed, most UR heroes have fewer than ten, and Nicole and Billy get 4. Some more shards sit in the Wishing Wheel and recruit lists, but that is a lottery, not a payout.

Arthur barely accumulates for free, about fifty guaranteed shards, but he is the cheapest UR for money. The $0.99 bundle gives 3 of his shards while every other UR gives one shard at that price, and the next tier gives 4 shards for $1.99. On top of that, the first top-up of any amount pays 140 of his shards at once.

One thing covers both of them: of the fifteen UR heroes only these two have no server age condition, while the other thirteen enter the list between day 7 and day 98. A budget squad is therefore worth building around them rather than around whoever dropped: time collects Marlena, a couple of dollars collect Arthur.

The "reachable UR" rows below always contain both, and all five best such squads are pure warrior. Marlena and Arthur are both warriors, and together with Celia, Lucius, Bella, Sivir or Claire they reach the full +20% faction bonus, the biggest multiplier in the game. It is a rare case where a budget squad gets something almost no top-tier squad does.

## Survivability beats damage, but a pure tank squad still loses

The main conclusion shows in five cases, all computed the same way, with the best squad's effectiveness taken as 100.

| Squad | Survivability | Damage per second | Effectiveness |
|---|---|---|---|
| Best overall: 3 tanks, 2 DPS heroes | 131.1M | 3.2M | 100 |
| Three tanks, a DPS hero and a support | 150.7M | 2.7M | 98 |
| Tanks and support, no DPS | 146.9M | 2.6M | 91 |
| Five tanks | 136.6M | 2.4M | 77 |
| Five DPS heroes | 18.0M | 3.4M | 15 |

The table needs careful reading. The formula gives survivability and damage no different weight: effectiveness is their product, and both factors are equal. The skew comes not from the formula but from how the game works. Five DPS heroes hit only slightly harder than the best squad, 3.4M against 3.2M, while living seven times shorter, because damage reduction multiplies the whole squad's HP at once, whereas damage adds up linearly. A squad with no reduction loses that multiplier entirely, and no amount of attack pays it back. Put simply, investing in survivability yields a bigger gain in this game than the same investment in damage.

The opposite extreme does not win either. Five tanks survive longer than the best squad, 136.6M against 131.1M, but they hit far less, 2.4M against 3.2M, and fall back to 77 in effectiveness. Damage reduction hits its cap anyway, and the fourth and fifth tanks add almost nothing to it, so a tank beyond the third trades damage for nothing.

Hence the working shape. The top fifty holds an even split, 14 squads each, between three tanks with two DPS heroes and three tanks with a DPS hero and a support, followed by four tanks with one DPS hero, 11 squads. A support stands in 25 of the top 50 squads, and in 21 of them it is Harper. Among DPS heroes Marlena, Annie, Joker and Red Lady appear most often: they earn the place better than the rest.

## Who cannot be replaced

This is the direct answer to who to invest in first. For every hero the search was repeated without them, and the best squad from the rest was compared with the best squad with everyone available, taken as 100. The bigger the drop, the less replaceable the hero.

| Hero | Role | Best squad without them |
|---|---|---|
| Arthur | tank | 64 |
| Billy | tank | 98 |
| Ulfrid | tank | 98 |
| Annie | DPS | 98 |
| Joker | DPS | 99 |

The gap speaks for itself. Without Arthur the best available squad reaches 64 out of 100: he alone provides the biggest shield in the game and two sources of damage reduction at once. The rest have stand-ins: without Billy, Ulfrid or Annie 98 remains, without Joker 99. Squad-wide damage reduction comes from three heroes at once, Billy, Ulfrid and Louis, so the squad barely notices when one of them drops out.

For a player that means something simple: investing in Arthur pays better than investing in anyone else, and he is also the cheapest UR for money.

## Who to keep on the bench

The support skill works while the hero stands outside the main squad, and every hero in the game has one. For UR heroes it gives +20% ATK, HP and DEF to the whole team and +10% skill cooldown speed, for SSR +10%, for SR +5%. The skill unlocks at 40 stars and level 30. Spare UR heroes are therefore never idle: they sit on the bench for that bonus.

## How to unlock the third squad

The word "squad" covers two different things in the game, and a question about a third squad is almost always about the second of them. The five heroes discussed above are picked at any moment and need no buildings at all. Standing apart from them, the city holds four buildings named Squad 1, Squad 2, Squad 3 and Squad 4, which the game labels march queues. Each of them adds one queue: one more group of five that can be outside the walls at the same time as the others.

Queues open by Sanctuary level, and the fourth one also asks for an active monthly pass. The table shows which building gives which queue and when it becomes available.

| Building | Queue | Unlocks at |
|---|---|---|
| Squad 1 | first | Sanctuary level 4 |
| Squad 2 | second | Sanctuary level 8 |
| Squad 3 | third | Sanctuary level 20 |
| Squad 4 | fourth | Sanctuary level 5 plus an active monthly pass |

The third queue costs the most in time: growing the Sanctuary to level 20 takes a long while, and until then the city keeps two marches outside the walls. Squad 4 is visible as early as level 5, yet the queue stays shut without the subscription, so plans for development are better made without it.
