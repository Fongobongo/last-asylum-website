Triangle Arena is the 3v3 formation duel: you set **three defense lineups**, and every attacker **picks which of your lineups to fight** — they must **win 2 of 3** to beat you. One rule shapes the entire event before anything else: **the attacker SEES your setups**. Defense in this game is not about being unbeatable; it is about not being readable.

This guide covers the defense principles, the sacrifice matchup rule for attacking, counter matchups, faction bonuses, and the Arena of Fate trap-squad strategy.

## The Format, and What It Means {#format}

- Defenders set **3 lineups**. Attackers choose the order they fight them in and must **win 2 of 3**.
- **The attacker sees all three of your teams** — this is the attacker's advantage, and every defense rule below exists to blunt it.

> You cannot stop a stronger player from beating you. You can stop a weaker player from farming you.

## Defense: Keep All Three Even {#defense-principles}

The single most common defense mistake: stacking your best heroes into one lineup. The attacker simply **avoids your strong team and beats the other two** — you lose 2-1 with your best squad never having fought. The rules:

- **Keep all 3 lineups EVEN.** Spread strength so there is no free win on the board.
- **Each team gets one of your 3 best tanks** in front — a good frontline is what makes an average lineup annoying to clear.
- **The weaker team gets the stronger heroes** — prop up your worst slot, not your best.
- **Put a second tank behind** the frontline — depth behind the first wall stretches every fight.
- **Power is not everything** — comp and counters decide fights; tune by trial and error, not by CP number.
- **Monitor who wins and loses against you** — the defense log is free tuning data. Rebalance when drift appears.

What even defense actually buys you: it does not stop stronger players, but it **prevents weaker ones from dropping your rank** — and rank defense is where the season's rewards live.

## The Sacrifice Matchup Rule {#sacrifice-rule}

The matchup principle that wins attacks:

> Vs a stacked defense, put your WORST squad into their BEST — then win the other two free.

Read their board, find the one lineup you cannot beat, and **concede it deliberately**. Your sacrifice squad exists to lose to their monster while your two better squads take the two winnable fights. The attacker chooses the order — never waste your best squad on their best lineup.

## Attack: Counters and Faction Bonuses {#attack-rules}

- **Your 10 best heroes are interchangeable across the top two teams.** Build the two wins, don't hoard heroes for a third fight you plan to lose anyway.
- **Counter matchups decide fights:** your rangers into their ranger/warrior lineups; your warriors into their warrior/warlock lineups.
- **Avoid their strongest lineup** — the sacrifice rule above. Only fight what you can beat.
- **Faction bonuses are key** — and because **you can see their defense**, you can pre-build your counter's faction bonus before you queue. The defender cannot adjust; you can.

Hero-by-hero counter picks are in the [Heroes guide](/heroes/).

## What This Achieves in Practice {#results}

Verified results from players running this system: **consistent top-20/40/100 finishes on mid rosters, up from 250+ before**. The arena rewards consistency over whale power — a readable account bleeds rank every day to attackers who see the weak lane; an even, monitored account holds it.

## Arena of Fate: The Trap Squad Strategy {#arena-of-fate}

- The special arena variant runs the same 2-of-3 format — and the **"Trap Squad"** play exploits the attacker's visibility advantage in reverse: bait balanced-looking squads that punch far above their apparent weight.
- Done well, it **grabs high-tier rewards, including UR shards** — the rare free UR source in PvP content.
- **Known bug: some players get stuck unranked on new servers** — if your rank never populates in a Fate season, it is the bug, not your play. Report and wait it out.
- **The greedy variant (Korpez):** build three squads as *strong main / decoy / trap* — the **trap squad shows tiny visible Might while hiding your 10★ carry** (Marlena, Cynthia). Most players scan Might numbers, don't check compositions, so they waste their attack on the "easy" slot and eat the full carry. Extreme version: **run a 4-man formation** with a tank removed — the apparent power drop baits even mid-tier players into attacking.
- "Trap squad + tank removed" took Korpez and his ally from rank ~160 into the top 50 without extra spending. Visibility of hero power trumps displayed Might.

## 📚 Reference: Arenas: battles, rewards and rank {#wiki-arenas}

Source: [wiki-last-asylum.com](https://wiki-last-asylum.com/en/wiki/arenas), client v1.0.87.


Arenas turn hero Might into steady income: every battle pays Antitoxin, and every season closes with a diamond payout by rank. There are several arenas, differing in entry, battle count and rewards. The lowest one opens almost immediately, while the higher ones ask for a strong rank in hero Might. Nearly all of the income comes from the free battles, and bought ones return far less than they cost.

## Battles and rewards

The number of free battles depends on the arena, and the spread between them is sixfold.

| Arena | Free battles per day |
|---|---|
| Lowest | 5 |
| Higher ones | 10 each |
| Separate one, no top-ups | 30 |

A win in the lowest arena gives 50,000 Antitoxin, a loss gives 25,000, plus glory medals. Five free battles come to 250,000 Antitoxin a day, and Antitoxin goes straight into hero levels. The source is steady and costs nothing beyond the five battles themselves.

## Season rewards: diamonds by rank

Beyond the daily battles, arenas pay out by season rank, and they pay in diamonds. One-off sources such as boss tasks, growth goals and Sanctuary level rewards total more, yet they run dry, while the arena pays again every season. Qualifying is easier than it looks, since the payouts reach down to the thousandth places. The table holds the extremes for each arena.

| Arena | First place | Bottom payout |
|---|---|---|
| Lower (two) | 1,010 diamonds | 85 diamonds down to rank 15,000 |
| Middle | 1,000 diamonds and 5,000 skill badges | 50 diamonds and 1,000 badges |
| Upper | 4,000 diamonds and 21,000 skill badges | 200 diamonds and 3,000 badges |
| Top | 15,200 diamonds | 3,000 diamonds down to rank 200 |

The top arena has only ten reward steps and two hundred places, yet its last place still pays three thousand diamonds, more than first place on a lower arena.

The skill badges from the middle and upper arenas matter on their own terms: 21,000 badges is an eighth of a full SR skill, which costs 170,200, and a twentieth of an SSR or UR skill at 436,700.

## Might needed for a rank

Arena ranks are not held by live players alone. Trainer bots with fixed Might sit among them, and they show what a squad needs to reach a given place.

| Rank | Opponent Might |
|---|---|
| 1 | 4,372,700 |
| 3 | 3,587,400 |
| 10 | 2,926,300 |
| 50 | 2,647,394 |
| 100 | 2,265,290 |
| 300 | 1,325,315 |
| 500 | 957,000 |
| 1000 | 509,524 |
| 2000 | 247,776 |

Might nearly halves between rank 300 and rank 100, so climbing out of the third hundred into the first costs roughly a million Might. Higher up the table each place costs more than the one below it.

## Refreshes and challenges

The opponent list refreshes three times a day for free, and further refreshes are paid: up to ten in the lower and middle arenas, five in the upper one. The top arena has no refreshes at all, since it hands out thirty battles a day and none of them can be bought.

The lowest arena adds a separate rule for challenges from the rank table. Only a player inside the top ten may issue one, and the target has to stand no lower than rank fifty. Everyone else fights the opponents the arena picks.

## Buying extra battles

Not every arena allows top-ups. Where it does, each purchased battle costs more than the last: 100, 200, 300, 400 diamonds and then 500 apiece. Five bought battles come to 1,500 diamonds and return nothing close to that.

## What it means in practice

Hero Might converts into resources here directly, and a higher rank opens the arenas with the larger rewards. The free battles deliver that income at no cost, which is why they are taken every day, even on days with no appetite for fighting. Which squad to field against a particular opponent from the list is suggested by the arena team builder: a battle simulation runs through the marked heroes and shows the five with the best outcome.

## Triangle Arena Field Notes: Might Lies, Factions and Journals (DTF) {#dtf-field-notes}

You can lose to a weaker player and beat someone millions stronger minutes later: three squads fight, two wins take it. Sometimes the best move is never touching the enemy's best squad; sometimes one hero outweighs a million might; sometimes five gold frames collapse because the main carry has a hollow sword.

![Triangle Arena tips and secrets](/img/dtf/arena/01.jpg)

### Defense stays even, attack reshuffles every fight {#dtf-defense}

Stacking everything expensive (top tank, carry, support, second DD) into squad 1 gifts the attacker two free wins: they feed the weak squad and take the other two 2:1. Spread so no free wins exist — a strong Marlena doesn't need every best Warrior beside her; moving one star (e.g. Harper to squad 3, Celia covering support elsewhere) denies the easy 2. Re-check defense after every big upgrade so squad 1 doesn't bloat while the tail starves.

![Three defensive squads on Triangle Arena](/img/dtf/arena/03.jpg)

Defense is set-and-hold; **attack rebuilds almost every serious fight**. First read the enemy board: with them at 18/13/12M and you at ~13–15M, don't fight the 18M — feed it your weakest squad and win 13 and 12. Chasing all three wastes attempts.

![Three battles with 2-of-3 win condition](/img/dtf/arena/04.jpg)

### Might is a hint, factions flip close fights {#dtf-might}

A 12M team with developed Marlena/Arthur, a real carry, skills and faction fit routinely beats a 14M pile of tanks and support that kills nothing. Always check who the number is made of — especially Marlena, who solo-carries games — before feeding a "10M" team your second squad.

![Might vs composition comparison](/img/dtf/arena/05.jpg)

Then check factions: **Rangers > Warriors > Warlocks > Rangers**, about **+20%** for the counter — enough to eat a solid might gap (your slightly stronger Warriors into Rangers is their fight, not yours; vs strong Rangers try Warlocks). Glance at faction icons every fight when the gap is under a million. But don't assemble a Frankenstein for the bonus: a counter is good only as a working team of that faction — three factions inside one squad with no formation bonus already lost its author a might-up matchup once.

### Post-battle compare, gear and trap numbers {#dtf-compare}

Always open the post-fight development compare — heroes, troops, tech, Raven, relics, Hall of Honor and more. A lopsided bar names the fix (better heroes and troops but lagging Raven/Hall means the next upgrade is obvious; one author's gap was Hall 31 vs 45). Gold frames kill nobody: a strong hero with a weak sword, low skills and hollow gear loses to a properly built carry. Marlena and Zoe show it most — one maxed Zoe decided a game alone, while several half-built attackers achieved nothing. Rule: **one real DD over three almost-DDs**; if Marlena carries, her attacking gear (e.g. sword toward 60, 4-star) eats first, not a showcase sword on a level-30 Arthur. Grow one main DD per key faction rather than nine medium attackers.

![Post-battle compare across heroes, troops, tech, Raven, relics](/img/dtf/arena/07.jpg)

Never auto-mark the weakest number as free: a 10–12M squad hiding Marlena + Arthur is a trap — every squad looks weaker on paper yet two fights lose on heroes, factions and inner growth. Same for troops: good heroes with paid faces but **T8 into T9** is already a visible gap, and sometimes the next army tier beats another hero star.

![Weak-looking squad hiding Marlena](/img/dtf/arena/08.jpg)

![T8 troops vs T9](/img/dtf/arena/09.jpg)

The mirror: an opponent with ~20M in squad 1 and two weak tails should be ignored at squad 1 entirely — take the two tails and beat a scarier total. Even defense beats one super-squad for exactly this reason.

![Opponent with one super-squad and two weak tails](/img/dtf/arena/10.jpg)

### Journal, pacing, matchmaking and freebies {#dtf-journal}

The **Journal** shows incoming (blue) vs outgoing (red) attacks — free defense testing. Compare same-might attackers: one farms your two weak squads, another loses at equal power through bad splits. If defense keeps donating the same two squads, the problem is found.

![Defense journal](/img/dtf/arena/11.jpg)

Don't burn all attempts in the morning: ranks drift as others attack (170 → 400+ in hours is normal). Take dailies to your rough ceiling, then re-climb in the evening with saved attempts. Early-week matchmaking is odd before ratings settle (30M, then 52M, then someone weak) — it keys on rating coins (105 meeting 108, 216 meeting 217), not real power, so use day one for experiments. Dailies: **3 and 5 wins** needn't be streaks and pay Glory Medals; ranked/alliance rewards include building skins (e.g. +5% hero attack); **likes pay 30 diamonds** (up to 180/day across two arenas) — never skip free diamonds.

Every fight: find the most dangerous enemy squad, pick two real targets, re-check factions and hidden carries (a pumped Marlena in the "weak" slot resets the math), shuffle Bella/Harper/Claire/Celia between squads, and only then look at might. Slower, and that's why it's interesting.
