The Raven is the system that quietly separates accounts: it works like an extra hero for all your squads at once, and its epigraphs are where the mono-faction meta is actually enforced. The Raven is also the system most players neglect — a Raven stuck at level 1 is a classic mistake.

This page covers Raven levels and plans, gear strategy, epigraph rarities and the promotion order, and the rules that decide which epigraphs deserve materials.

## What the Raven Is {#what-raven-is}

The Raven is an extra hero for all your squads — its buffs apply everywhere at once, not just to one formation. Two consequences follow:

- Every point of Raven investment improves every march you field, which makes it one of the highest-value systems in the game.
- Crow Essence, its food, is the bottleneck on the entire system. Farm it early and never stop — the VIP shop and Sanctuary shop both sell it for exactly this reason.

The long-term goal is **Raven level 110**, where the final skill lands: +50% AOE damage to affected targets. That is the endgame of the system, and Crow Essence is what stands between you and it.

## Raven Levels and Plans {#raven-levels}

Raven levels unlock the epigraph slots (called Plans) one at a time:

| Raven level | Unlock |
|---|---|
| 10 | Epigraphs + Plan 1 (first slot) |
| 150 | Plan 2 (+1 system bonus), new Raven look & stats |
| 200 | New Raven look & enhanced stats |
| 250 | New endgame look & maximum base stats |
| 300 | Plan 3 (+2) |
| 450 | Plan 4 (+3) |
| 600 | Plan 5 (+4) |

Read the table as the Raven's true progression path: level 10 opens the whole epigraph system, and every 150 levels after that adds another slot to fill. Reaching levels 150, 200, and 250 also unlocks new visual looks and major stat boosts. The interface now features separate buttons for Skin selection and Upgrade Preview. Because each Plan slot carries its own epigraph buff, a stalled Raven level caps how many of those buffs you can even equip.

## Raven Gear Strategy {#raven-gear}

Push Raven gear to level 8 — that is where the percentage buffs arrive, and percentage buffs are what make the gear worth wearing.

Side strategy, left versus right:

- **Left side** — HP and crit-damage-reduction. Easier to acquire, defensive value.
- **Right side** — ATK and crit-damage. The best offensive value in the system.

And one calendar rule: **save your gear chests for Alliance Duel days.** Opening them then earns Alliance Duel points — the same double-dip logic that governs speedups in the [Economy guide](/economy/).

## Epigraphs: The Core Rules {#epigraph-basics}

Epigraphs slot into the Raven's Plans and buff the heroes of a faction. The rarities behave differently, and the differences drive every upgrade decision:

- **UR (gold)** — buffs ONE faction only. The end goal, and the reason mono-faction squads are the meta. As of the August 20, 2026 update, Raven Epigraphs can now be upgraded up to **6 Stars** (previously capped at 5★), unleashing massive percentage stat scaling.

- **SR (blue) and R (green)** — buff ALL factions, but cannot be promoted. They are the everyone-gets-a-cut tier, useful while you build toward UR.

The single most important rule: **only upgrade epigraphs of your main squad's faction.** A UR epigraph that buffs a faction you do not field is worth nothing to you — and in the mono-faction meta, off-faction materials are exactly what the feed stock is for (see Quick Add below).

### The Early Meta Epigraphs {#early-epigraphs}

| Epigraph | Slot type | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Frenzy Flock | Attack | ATK buff to Warrior allies after the Raven skill fires, plus more Raven targets. |
| Reversal Power | Support | Damage for the 3 highest-ATK Warrior allies, and reduces enemy ultimate damage. |

## The Epigraph Workshop {#epigraph-workshop}

Crafting UR epigraphs requires **Workshop level 20**. Everything below that is building toward it.

The rarity ladder, and what each color is actually for:

| Color | Role | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Green | Material | The feedstock tier. |
| Blue | Improved material | An SR blue with 2 stars performs roughly like an SSR purple — use blues until you outgrow them. |
| Purple | Transition tier | Good enough to fight with while your URs grow. |
| Gold (UR) | End goal | The target of the entire system. |

One absolute rule: **never recycle a golden epigraph unless you are certain.** UR epigraphs are the scarcest permanent asset in the game — a recycled UR is not coming back.

## Epigraph Promotion Order {#promotion-order}

Follow this order (from DrAction), promoting one slot at a time:

1. Mid — Defense slot (damage reduction).
2. Top — Attack slot (Raven Target).
3. Right — Support slot (damage reduction).
4. Left — Initiative slot (Defense% buff).

For Warrior sword epigraphs specifically, the community variant: Support (right) UR first, then Attack (top), then Initiative or Defense.

## Rarity Comparisons That Surprise People {#rarity-comparisons}

Star level can beat rarity in the damage slot. These are the verified comparisons players argue about most:

- A purple at 4 stars can outdamage a yellow (UR) at 0 stars in the attack slot — the community shorthand is purple DMG all the way.
- A shield UR at 0 stars is better than an SSR at 5 stars.
- Purple outperforms UR against King Blight until the UR reaches 3 stars or better.

The takeaway is not that purple is secretly the best tier — it is that a high-star epigraph of the right type beats a fresh UR of the wrong one. Star your epigraphs before you chase their rarity ceiling.

## SSR, UR, and What Actually Upgrades {#ssr-vs-ur}

A common misunderstanding worth killing early: you **cannot upgrade an SSR epigraph into a UR**. What you upgrade is the Plan — the slot the epigraph sits in. SSR epigraphs are placeholders in that progression, not embryos.

Which leads to the most common Raven mistake in the game: players hoard non-main-faction epigraphs instead of feeding them to the Raven via **Quick Add** — and then wonder why their Raven is stuck at level 1. Off-faction and low-rarity epigraphs are Raven food. Quick Add them; that is what they are for.

## 📚 Reference: Falcon Quests: types and Sanctuary level {#wiki-falcon-tasks}

Source: [wiki-last-asylum.com](https://wiki-last-asylum.com/en/wiki/falcon-tasks), client v1.0.87.

Falcon Quests are handed out by the Falcon Tower itself: the list refreshes and the player picks from it. Each quest carries a quality from 1 to 5, quality decides the size of the reward, and there are 8 hours to finish. The point worth knowing is that which quests show up depends on the Sanctuary level, so city progress changes the draw itself and not only the payout.

## Quest types

The set of quests is fixed, and they differ in what they cost in energy and what they bring back.

| Task | Energy | Drops |
|---|---|---|
| Efficient Gathering | free | diamonds, grain, herbs |
| Blight Extermination | 10 | Raven Fruit, Resource Supply (General), herbs |
| Defeat Blight Legion | 20 | Raven Fruit, Resource Supply (General), grain |
| Pirate Legacy | free | Resource Supply (General), Lv.1 Raven Gear Chest, Raven Essence |
| Aid Alliance Members | 10 | Resource Supply (General), grain, herbs |

| Rescue Refugee | free | Nurse Alice Shard, Attendant Lancelot Shard, Explorer Dorota Shard |
| Celia's Trial 1 | free | 1K Antitoxin, Skill Badge, hero XP |

## Sanctuary level decides what shows up

Quest quality is random, but the weights depend on the difficulty level, which maps one to one to the Sanctuary level. Difficulty cannot be raised on its own, as it follows the territory. At level 1, quality four is 18% of rolls and quality five 9%. At level 35, quality four is 41% and quality five 16%.

The Sanctuary therefore does more than raise the reward per quest: it reshapes the draw, and the expensive quests start turning up several times as often. That is one more reason not to stall it, and the game never spells it out.

Half the quests cost no energy at all, namely gathering, supply recovery, refugee rescue and digs, which makes them worth taking every time. Only combat quests cost energy, 10 for hunts and 20 for rally targets.

## How many quests the tower holds

The Falcon Tower refreshes every 6 hours: a new batch of quests lands in storage, and a quest moves to the visible list once a slot there frees up. How many quests are visible, how many wait in storage and how many arrive per refresh depend on the tower level, and the table shows what each level gives. The level grows with the number of rewards claimed, and level fifteen takes 5000 quests.

## Whether stockpiling pays

Stockpiling quests pays only for the Alliance Duel: on days one, three and five every Falcon Quest handed in brings 10 000 points. Players complete a quest but leave the reward unclaimed, and it stays in the visible list with a red dot until the right day. By player observation a completed quest never burns, an uncompleted one disappears after 8 hours, and Wandering Blight quests do not count against storage.

The ceiling of such a stockpile is the visible quests plus the storage, and the storage fills in under a day: 30 hours at tower level one, 18-24 hours from level five on. After that every refresh is wasted, while the tower hands out from 24 quests a day at level one to 52 at level fifteen. Stockpiling longer than a day therefore does not pay: on duel days two and four quests are completed and kept, on days one, three and five everything is handed in, and at the weekend rewards are claimed at once. Stockpiling does not affect the tower level, it only delays it.

## Raven Leveling Without Mistakes: Workshop, Nest, Epigraphs, Routine {#raven-dtf-leveling}

Field notes from the DTF leveling guide: the Raven lives in three places, not one — and order matters more than speed.

**The three buildings.** The Raven Workshop is background production: it generates materials over time with no flashy payoff, but progress sags without it. The Raven Nest is the main screen — level, evolution, new stages and squad bindings. The Epigraph Workshop is where most wrong choices happen: one bad craft or slot decision either lives with you as a weak boost or costs a reset.

![Raven system overview](/img/dtf/raven/01.jpg)

**Levels vs evolution.** The Raven levels on Raven Fruit and evolves on Raven Essence, with an evolution step every 10 levels unlocking the next layer of progress. Essence is the binding constraint: Fruit arrives steadily through routine play, Essence always feels tight. Watch the next evolution threshold first — levels without their evolution step hit a ceiling fast.

![Raven hero screen](/img/dtf/raven/02.jpg)

**Gear: build the base, then sharpen.** Do not open chests and slam upgrades as they drop. Assemble the full set evenly first, then merge duplicates and raise gear deliberately. Some upgrades are sequence-gated — other slots must reach a level before a piece opens — so maxing one slot early leaves holes elsewhere. Treat gear as a kit, not a lottery.

![Raven gear screen](/img/dtf/raven/03.jpg)

**Daily sources.** Check every day: Alliance shop (assorted Raven materials), VIP shop (Essence and chests for diamonds), Expedition shop (higher-level Raven chests), Honor shop (rare late-stage chests), Sanctuary shop (Essence on rotation). Buy only what closes your current task — sweeping every item labeled "Raven" produces inventory without progress. Name the shortage explicitly before spending: Epigraph Workshop materials, or duplicates for merging.

**Epigraph thresholds.** Purple epigraphs open at Epigraph Workshop level 15 — before that you are not really in the system. UR epigraphs come later, on the next workshop stage with more expensive materials. Epigraph bonuses apply only to the matching squad type: if your main squad is Warriors, crafting Ranger epigraphs "for the future" burns scarce materials for zero current return. Pick the faction you actually field, fill one set, and leave side sets alone until the core is done. Inside a set the slots differ (attack / defense / support / general center) — fix the faction first, then tune by need: lacking damage means pushing the attack slot, a collapsing squad means defense, and the general slot means an even boost.

![Epigraphs as the strongest and most dangerous part of the system](/img/dtf/raven/04.jpg)

![Epigraph slots for Warriors, Rangers and Sorcerers](/img/dtf/raven/05.jpg)

**Duplicates are fuel.** A second copy of the right epigraph is not a bad drop — identical epigraphs raise stars on the existing piece, and for UR pieces exact duplicates of the right item are the most valuable resource in the line. Wrong-type duplicates wait or go through reset redistribution; right-type duplicates get starred.

![Duplicate epigraphs as fuel for growth](/img/dtf/raven/06.jpg)

**Evolution opens squads.** Evolution is not just stats: it unlocks additional squad slots. You start with only the first squad, the second slot opens at the next evolution stage, and requirements keep rising. That is another reason to commit to your strongest squad early — there is physically nowhere to spread at the start.

![Squad slots unlocked by Raven evolution](/img/dtf/raven/07.jpg)

**Reset exists.** Bad crafts are recoverable: reset rolls back an upgrade and returns the materials for redistribution. It is most useful when you switch main squad class, outgrow a temporary squad, or picked the wrong epigraph at the start. It lowers the cost of a mistake; it does not make blind crafting free.

**Daily routine.** Check the shops, keep the next evolution in mind, develop only the epigraph type of your main squad, and reset mistakes instead of living with them. Played as a daily loop the Raven compounds; played in bursts it feels expensive and vague.
