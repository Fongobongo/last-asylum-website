Might alone misleads: a higher number can still lose when the squad covers its damage dealers badly or misunderstands how the fight unfolds. Five heroes do not hit the nearest target all at once — the squad is split into two groups, each position has a job, and the battle runs in three phases with shifting targets as fighters fall.

![How battle and formation work](/img/dtf/combat/01.jpg)

## Squad Positions: 1-1..1-5 and Two Battle Groups {#positions}

Each five has five slots. Squad one uses **1-1, 1-2, 1-3, 1-4, 1-5**; squad two uses 2-1..2-5. The first digit is the squad number, the second the hero slot (e.g. 1-3 is slot three of squad one).

Each five is further split into two combat groups:

- **1-MU (main unit):** positions 1-1, 1-3, 1-4.
- **1-SU (support unit):** positions 1-2, 1-5.
- Mirrored: **2-MU** = 2-1, 2-3, 2-4; **2-SU** = 2-2, 2-5.

By the battle rules **1-MU opens against enemy position 2-1, and 1-SU against 2-2** (mirrored for the opponent). Hence the standard layout:

| Position | Role |
|---|---|
| **1-1** | Main tank (takes its group's opening pressure) |
| **1-2** | Second tank |
| **1-3** | Main damage dealer |
| **1-4** | Second damage dealer |
| **1-5** | Support |

A carry on 1-1 "because of high Might" takes pressure early and can die before casting — put tanks there instead.

## Phase 1: Both Groups Open Separately {#phase-1}

At the start the five still holds its formation: a three-hero main group and a two-hero support group, each fighting on its side.

![Phase 1: main and support groups split](/img/dtf/combat/02.jpg)

The main tank on 1-1 stands ahead of two attackers; while it lives, 1-3 and 1-4 get time to deal damage. The 1-2 tank does the same for the 1-5 support.

![Position map 1-1..1-5 and 2-1..2-5 in phase 1](/img/dtf/combat/03.jpg)

Rule: two defenders take the opening hit, two damage dealers hit from behind them, support assists the support group. If the five is right but a tank still cannot hold its side, that specific tank is the upgrade target — not everything at once.

## Why Tank Survival Is Carry Damage {#tank-buys-time}

A familiar case: the carry is leveled, weaponed and starred, yet battle damage disappoints. Before feeding it more, check how long the tank in front lives.

If 1-1 drops almost instantly, the main group loses cover and 1-3/1-4 take pressure early and never reach full potential. Then one more tank upgrade helps the team more than another carry upgrade — the tank literally buys time for the two heroes behind it. If the front holds confidently but the enemy still survives, work on 1-3/1-4 (skills, stars, attack gear).

## Phase 2: First Slots Fall, the Fight Reshapes {#phase-2}

After the first losses the starting scheme changes: survivors take new targets, previously covered heroes move closer to the enemy.

![Phase 2 begins after the first hero losses](/img/dtf/combat/04.jpg)

This is where front-line mistakes surface. A weak main tank sends its group into phase 2 damaged, with damage dealers already under fire. A front that held makes the transition cheap: the second line keeps HP and keeps pressing.

![Phase 2 continues as more heroes fall](/img/dtf/combat/05.jpg)

Think of it as a chain, not five duels: hero drops, the rest shift. Lose a tank, the carries open. Remove the enemy tank first, your attackers reach the next targets sooner — focused damage snowballs.

### Who to check after a phase-2 loss {#phase-2-diagnosis}

Do not start from total Might. Ask who left too early: 1-1 falling first → strengthen the main tank; trouble on the other side → 1-2; both tanks holding but a carry vanishing right after → that carry's build and gear. There is little use adding damage to Marlena when her group collapses because the tank ahead dies at the start — and little use armoring a tank forever when the team already lives long but cannot finish.

## Phase 3: Whoever Survived Fights On {#phase-3}

By phase 3 the starting scheme is gone: groups are smaller, and the remaining heroes fight whoever survived on the other side.

![Phase 3: remaining heroes and attack directions](/img/dtf/combat/06.jpg)

This is where whole-squad quality shows. A main carry that reaches phase 3 alive keeps dealing high damage to what is left; a surviving support still helps. A glass-cannon build — one huge attacker behind two abandoned tanks — often ends alone. The carry must be strong, but first it must live to the part of the fight where strength matters.

### Phase-based upgrade diagnosis {#upgrade-diagnosis}

- Squad crumbles immediately → tanks (level, armor/boots, defensive skills).
- Phase 1 survived, attackers lost fast in phase 2 → their positions, skills and gear.
- Reaches phase 3 steadily but cannot kill → main carry focus.

Pick the moment control was lost, then spend the next shard, badge or gear set there.

## Troops Are Half the March {#troops-matter}

Heroes are one half of the squad — soldiers march with them, and count plus tier matter. Two players with similar heroes and the same formation can differ purely on troops. If heroes look right but equal-level opponents keep winning, check march fullness, available soldier tier and Training Grounds development before blaming heroes.

![Troops decide fights too](/img/dtf/combat/07.jpg)

## Casualty Rules: Wounded Are Not Fallen {#casualty-rules}

After battle, some troops are heavily wounded (healable) and some die permanently (retrained). The split depends on the mode — know the price of defeat before a serious attack.

![Wounded vs fallen rates by mode](/img/dtf/combat/08.jpg)

| Mode | Heavily wounded | Fallen (permanent) |
|---|---|---|
| **Trade Caravan** | 0% | 0% — test new formations here |
| **Territory capture (attacker)** | 60% | **20% permanent** |
| **Assault defense (defender)** | 60% | 0% |

60% wounded looks scary but comes back. 20% fallen must be retrained — painful across long alliance-war series, where winning one fight can eat the army for the next ones. The attacker's mistake costs notably more than the defender's.

### Pre-fight checklist {#pre-fight-checklist}

Tanks on 1-1 and 1-2 in place and developed? → 1-3/1-4 really the best damage dealers, not accidental high-Might picks? → march full with the right tier? → how much army is permanently lost if this mode is lost? A few seconds here saves more than another small Might bump.
