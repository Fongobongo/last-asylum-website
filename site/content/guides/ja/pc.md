Last Asylum on PC runs only through an Android emulator: the game is released on iOS and Android, and the publisher offers no Windows, Mac or console version. The emulator installs the ordinary build from Google Play, and the same account as on the phone signs in there, so the city continues from where it was. The facts were checked on 9 September 2026 against the game's store pages and the emulator sites.

## There is no official desktop version

The game's pages on Google Play and the App Store remain the only official sources, and the publisher's site links nowhere else. Files named "Last Asylum for Windows" or "PC client" on third-party sites have nothing to do with the game and are best left alone: other programs travel under that name.

## Which emulators work

The game is listed by the two most common emulators: both BlueStacks and LDPlayer keep a dedicated install page, both run on Windows, and BlueStacks also runs on macOS. The steps are the same: install the emulator, sign in to Google Play with the usual account, find the game and install it as on a phone. Players who keep several characters pick BlueStacks for its multiple windows, while weaker computers do better with LDPlayer and its smaller memory footprint.

## Moving the city from the phone

A character lives in the publisher's account, not on the device. Until the account is bound it exists only on the phone, and signing in from a computer creates a new character. The order is this: first bind the account on the phone to Google or another external login through the platform window in the game settings, then sign in inside the emulator with the same login and pick the server from the list. Binding, switching and deleting the account are covered on the Account page, and what happens when another server is chosen on the Changing server page.

## Worth knowing in advance

Playing on a computer is no different from playing on a phone: the same servers, the same events and the same shop, with purchases going through Google Play. The game rules forbid programs that play on the player's behalf, and neighbours' reports of such activity end in a chat or account block, so the emulator serves as a big screen and a keyboard rather than a substitute for the player. Where to begin on a new server is described on the First days page.
